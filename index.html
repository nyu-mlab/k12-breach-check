Major vendor incidents

Some of the largest K-12 incidents happened at education technology vendors, not at individual districts. If your district used one of these products, its data may have been exposed through the vendor.

What this is, and what it is not

What this is

A public index of reported cybersecurity incidents at the school or district level, compiled from public records: state attorney general filings, news reporting, vendor disclosures, and research datasets. If your school or district appears here, at least one incident tied to it has been publicly reported.

What it is not

We track incidents per school, not per person. We do not hold email addresses, student lists, or any individual records from these breaches, so this site cannot tell you whether your personal information was exposed. For that, contact your district directly or watch for the notification letters districts are required to send after a breach.

How to read a result

Each incident card shows the date, the type of incident, how many records were reported affected (when a number was published), a confidence badge, the kinds of data reported exposed, and links to the public sources. Corroborated means multiple independent sources reported it. Reported means at least one credible source. Unconfirmed means the report could not be independently verified; treat it with caution.

Coverage limits

This index reflects what has been publicly reported, and reporting is uneven. Many incidents are never disclosed, so an empty result means no publicly reported incidents found, not that nothing ever happened.
